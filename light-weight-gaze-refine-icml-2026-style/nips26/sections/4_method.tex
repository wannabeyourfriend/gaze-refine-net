\section{Online per-trial shrinkage}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Setup.}
After the standard 18-point calibration phase, the user is shown $K$
extra targets and the eye tracker records the corresponding raw gaze
estimates $\{(\tilde p_j, p_j^\star)\}_{j=1}^K$. The classical
\emph{anchor} baseline (sim+RBF in our experiments) produces predictions
$\hat{a}_j = g_{\rm anchor}(\tilde p_j)$. The empirical per-trial bias
estimate from these $K$ samples is
\begin{equation}
\bar{b}_K \;=\; \frac{1}{K} \sum_{j=1}^{K} \big(p_j^\star - \hat{a}_j\big)
\;\in\; \mathbb{R}^2.
\label{eq:emp-bias}
\end{equation}
At test time, given a new raw gaze estimate $p$ and its anchor prediction
$a = g_{\rm anchor}(p)$, the corrected prediction is
\begin{equation}
\hat{p}_{\rm refined} \;=\; a \;+\; \boldsymbol{\lambda} \odot \bar{b}_K,
\label{eq:correction}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\lambda} = (\lambda_x, \lambda_y) \in [0, 1]^2$ is a
shrinkage coefficient and $\odot$ is the element-wise product.
$\boldsymbol{\lambda} = \boldsymbol{1}$ corresponds to subtracting the raw
empirical bias; $\boldsymbol{\lambda} = \boldsymbol{0}$ recovers the
classical anchor.

\paragraph{Why shrinkage is necessary.}
At small $K$, the variance of $\bar{b}_K$ is large relative to its mean,
and using it raw inflates error rather than reducing it. On our
self-collected data, raw mean-bias correction at $K = 1$ yields $58.8$~px
versus $44.7$~px for the anchor alone (Figure~\ref{fig:shrinkage}a, orange
line). Sweeping a single global $\lambda$ per $K$ gives clear
improvements: the optimal $\lambda$ rises monotonically from $0.2$ at
$K=1$ to $0.8$ at $K=18$, exactly the James-Stein pattern of less
shrinkage as the empirical mean becomes more reliable
(Figure~\ref{fig:shrinkage}b). However, a global $\lambda$ ignores
trial-specific reliability cues such as the variability of the bias
samples and their absolute magnitude.

\paragraph{Learned per-trial shrinkage.}
We train a small MLP to predict $\boldsymbol{\lambda}$ from per-trial bias
statistics. The features are:
\begin{equation}
\phi(K, \bar{b}_K, S_K) = \big[
\bar{b}_K / 50,\;
\log(1 + |\bar{b}_K| / 10),\;
S_K / 100,\;
\log(1 + S_K / 10),\;
{\rm range}_K / 100,\;
1/K,\;
\log K
\big],
\label{eq:features}
\end{equation}
where $S_K = \mathrm{Var}_j(\hat{a}_j - p_j^\star)$ is the per-coordinate
sample variance and ${\rm range}_K$ is the per-coordinate sample range.
The MLP has two hidden layers of width 32 with LayerNorm and GELU
activations, and outputs $\boldsymbol{\lambda} = \sigma(W_2 h)$ via a
sigmoid head initialized at zero (so an untrained model emits
$\boldsymbol{\lambda} = 0.5$). Total parameter count: $\approx 1\,200$.

\paragraph{Training.}
We train per leave-one-subject-out (LOSO) fold. Within each training
fold, we form mini-batches of episodes: each episode is a random
$(K, K')$ split of one trial's evaluation samples into a context of
size $K$ and a query of size $K'$. The loss on a single episode is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} \;=\; \frac{1}{K'} \sum_{j=1}^{K'}
\big\| (p_j^\star - \hat{a}_j) - \boldsymbol{\lambda}(K, \bar{b}_K, S_K) \odot \bar{b}_K \big\|_2^2
\label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
where $\bar{b}_K, S_K$ are computed from the context. We optimize with
AdamW (lr $3 \times 10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-3}$) for $80$ epochs,
selecting the best checkpoint on a held-out validation episode pool.

\paragraph{Deployment.}
At inference time, the user contributes $K$ extra fixations after the
standard calibration; $\bar{b}_K$ and $S_K$ are computed and passed
through $\phi$ and the MLP to obtain $\boldsymbol{\lambda}$. The
corrected prediction is given by \eqref{eq:correction}. The MLP is
trained once across the available training subjects and frozen
thereafter; no per-user training is required.
