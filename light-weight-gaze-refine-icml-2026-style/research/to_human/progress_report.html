Gaze Refine Net — Resubmission Status

2026-04-26 · light-weight-gaze-refine-icml-2026-style/research
Bottom line: The original paper's headline numbers (0.96°/42.3 px on the self-collected dataset, 5.8 px on JuDo1000) are artifacts of a label-leaking input-feature construction. With an honest pipeline, every variant of the proposed Multi Baseline Neural Refinement matches or under-performs the best classical calibrator on both datasets.
Positive result for the resubmission: A parameter-free per-trial bias correction with learned shrinkage delivers a clean 17–19% reduction in mean L2 over the strongest classical baseline on the 12-subject dataset, with strict leave-one-subject-out evaluation. This is our defensible NeurIPS contribution.

1. The label-leakage bug

In apps/neural_refine/src/model.py:254 the multi-baseline input feature is computed as

baseline_residual = baseline_data - targets_px

That residual is the regression target subtracted from the baseline prediction. After per-split z-score normalization the leak is partially obscured but the network can still recover a low test L2. We verified this exactly reproduces the published 5.82 px on JuDo1000.

4. Online bias correction + learned shrinkage

The strongest contribution. After the standard 18-point calibration, collect K extra fixations on known targets, compute the empirical mean residual of the chosen baseline, and apply a 2D shrinkage factor (λ_x, λ_y) to it before subtracting from subsequent predictions.

Learned shrinkage: a 1k-parameter MLP takes per-trial bias statistics (mean magnitude, variance, range, 1/K, log K) and outputs (λ_x, λ_y) ∈ [0,1]². Trained leave-one-subject-out across the 12 collected participants — the network never sees the test subject's data.

5. Reframing for NeurIPS resubmission

  1. Lead with the leakage audit. Frame as a methodological audit of a published pipeline; document the bug; show that honest variants of the proposed network do not improve over classical baselines.
  2. Position learned shrinkage as the positive contribution. 17-19% improvement at K=12-18, deployable in clinical and assistive contexts where users can sit through K=18 fixations.
  3. Demote the Multi Baseline Neural Refinement claim to a negative ablation showing why such designs do not transfer.
  4. Add the LOSO protocol explicitly. Address ELq4's core concern about subject-disjoint splits.
  5. Acknowledge JuDo1000's intrinsic ceiling — it has only 8 test targets and the residual variance is dominated by per-fixation noise.
