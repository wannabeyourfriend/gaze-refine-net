569      B. Experimental Details
570
571      B.1. Calibration Evaluation Details
572      This experiment follows a two-phase design: model-fitting and model-testing.
573
574      Model-Fitting Phase. An 18-point calibration grid is displayed (Figure 3a). Points turn red sequentially in random order.
575      Participants fixate on each red point for 6 seconds: the first 2 seconds allow target acquisition (we verified that ¿95% of
576      participants successfully locate the target within this window), and gaze data are recorded during the subsequent 4 seconds.
577      This yields dense calibration data for training baseline methods.
578      Model-Testing Phase. A 32-point testing grid is presented (Figure 3b) following the same protocol. This phase evaluates
579      generalization performance on unseen spatial locations. A trial concludes once all 32 test points are completed.
580
581      Baseline Training. For each valid trial, we train seven baseline calibration methods (affine, RBF, affine-RBF, and
582      polynomials of orders 2, 3, 4) on the 18-point fitting data. We compute calibration error on the fitting points to perform
583      accuracy-conditioned baseline selection, retaining the top-4 performing methods for each trial.
584      Neural Refinement Training. We construct a training dataset by pooling raw gaze predictions and baseline-predicted
585      offsets from all valid trials. During training, we apply noise-aware data augmentation: baseline offsets are corrupted with
586      Gaussian noise ϵ ∼ N (0, σ 2 I). The neural network learns to predict ground-truth calibration offsets from noisy baseline
587      predictions. We train using Adam optimizer with learning rate 0.0003 for 100 epochs with early stopping based on validation
588      error.
589
590      B.2. Real World User Experimental Protocol
591
592      This experiment evaluates practical effectiveness through an eye-tracking-based musical game, where improved gaze
593      accuracy directly impacts user performance.
594      Model-Fitting Phase. Identical to Experiment 1: participants complete 18-point calibration to train baseline methods and
595      neural refinement.
596
597      Game-Testing Phase. Seventeen circles representing musical notes (C3 to D5) are displayed (Figure 3c). After a 3-second
598      preparation period, a slowed segment (0.3× speed) of ”Dance of the Golden Snake” is played. As each note sounds, its
599      corresponding circle turns red. Participants must quickly fixate on the red circle.
600      Scoring. Each circle illumination is worth 1 point. The first 0.7 seconds after a circle turns red are allocated for target
601      localization and excluded from scoring. For the remaining illumination time, the score is proportional to fixation duration
602      within the circle boundary. Scores across all circles are summed for the total performance score. This scoring scheme
603      directly rewards gaze accuracy: systematic calibration errors cause fixations to fall outside target circles, reducing scores.

651      C.3. Evidence confirming that the participant was fixating on the target location after a 2-second delay
652      For each calibration point, the participant’s mean fixation position (which is the raw predicted point) and its standard
653      deviation were calculated from the 4-second data recording period. A circular area with a radius of 3 was defined around
654      this mean position as the stable fixation region. The moment at which the participant’s gaze entered and remained within
655      this region was identified as the time when the new target was successfully located. Based on data from over 2,000 collected
656      calibration points, a complementary cumulative distribution function analysis revealed that for over 95% of points, stable
657      fixation was achieved within 1.83 seconds. This finding validates the rationale behind setting the 2-second threshold in our
658      experimental design.
